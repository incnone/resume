About

I earned a PhD in differential geometry at the University of Michigan, where I taught calculus and published my thesis work on rank rigidity in Geometriae Dedicata. Since then I co-created and shipped Bean and Nothingness, a logic-puzzle game rated 98% positive on Steam, and spent a decade building the rating models and services that run a competitive gaming league.

I like reasoning carefully about hard, well-defined problems — a theorem, a replay system, a rating model. What connects my work is wanting to know exactly why something is true, and being willing to derive it by hand to find out. I am looking for work in higher-ed teaching, game programming, or quantitative and machine-learning engineering, and I am open to relocation across the US and Canada, including internships.

CoNDOR Rating & Matchmaking

Open source · Python / C++ / MySQL / PuLP · multiple seasons through 2022

A Bayesian rating and matchmaking system for a multi-league competitive season, rebuilt and refined over several seasons. I wrote a custom paired-comparison model — Gaussian priors plus binomial match evidence, fit by MAP estimation with hand-derived gradients and a hand-written optimizer — chose its prior variance by maximizing likelihood on held-out matches from an earlier season, and — judging normal tails too thin for real performance — gave it a heavier-tailed link function whose tail weight is fit to past results. Series forecasts account for correlated rating uncertainty, which meant patching a C++ Bayesian Elo engine to expose its covariance matrix. Weekly pairings are solved as an integer program that maximizes the entropy of the predicted result, so every match is as informative as possible.

On teaching

I taught calculus and assisted differential equations at Michigan, and I have tutored across topology, abstract algebra, differential equations, statistics, electricity & magnetism, and C++. My aim is always the same: get the student to the point where the next step feels inevitable rather than memorized. I care most about building the mental picture behind a definition — the geometry under an equation — because that is what survives the exam and transfers to the next problem.
