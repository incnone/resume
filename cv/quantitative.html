Modeling & quantitative work

Competitive Rating & Matchmaking Systems · CoNDOR · open source
multiple seasons, through 2022
github.com/incnone/condor_ratings
A Bayesian rating model, series-outcome forecasting, and automated matchmaking, rebuilt and refined across several league seasons (Python, C++, MySQL, PuLP)
  • Built and iterated on a Bayesian paired-comparison rating model: per-player Gaussian priors combined with binomial match evidence, fit by MAP estimation
  • Derived the log-likelihood gradient analytically and wrote the optimizer by hand — batch gradient ascent with L2 convergence tests on both parameter change and gradient norm, iteration and wall-clock limits, and log-likelihood tracing
  • Selected the prior variance by maximizing log-likelihood on held-out matches from a previous season — model selection against real data rather than hand-tuning
  • Judged a normal model’s tails too thin for real performance variation and designed a heavier-tailed link function — a mixture of an error function and a rational sigmoid whose tail weight is a hyperparameter fit to past match data (α = 0.42) — calibrated to agree with the Bradley–Terry model at a chosen reference rating
  • Patched Rémi Coulom’s C++ Bayesian Elo engine (bayeselo) to expose the covariance matrix it computes internally, then modeled two players’ ratings as jointly normal so series predictions account for correlated rating uncertainty
  • Converted ratings into calibrated win probabilities and full best-of-3 / best-of-5 outcome distributions, exported as JSON for downstream use
  • Automated weekly matchmaking as an integer linear program (PuLP/CBC) maximizing an information-theoretic objective — the entropy of the predicted result, penalized by rating gap — under per-player degree constraints
  • Added a local-search pass performing minimum-cost pairwise exchanges to enforce a minimum cycle length in the matchup graph, with unit tests
